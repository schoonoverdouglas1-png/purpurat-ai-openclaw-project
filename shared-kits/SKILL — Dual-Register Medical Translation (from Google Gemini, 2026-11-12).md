# SKILL FILE: DUAL-REGISTER MEDICAL TRANSLATION

## TITLE
Dual-Register Medical Translation (DRMT-01)

## PURPOSE
To translate complex, highly technical medical or scientific documentation simultaneously into two distinct functional registers:
1. **The Clinical/Technical Register:** Precise, uncompromised terminology intended for medical professionals, researchers, or legal/regulatory compliance.
2. **The Layperson/Accessible Register:** Clear, plain-language translation designed for patient comprehension, general audiences, or rapid operational briefs without loss of core clinical accuracy.

## WHEN TO USE IT
- When processing incoming foreign-language medical records, research papers, or triage notes that must be understood instantly by both tactical/medical operators and civilian/patient stakeholders.
- When generating dual-audience reports where ambiguity in either register could lead to operational failure or patient harm.
- When Captain Schoonover or Ember requires a high-fidelity bridge between esoteric medical nomenclature and actionable, plain-English comprehension.

## PROCEDURE (STEP BY STEP)
1. **Ingest and Segment:** Receive the source text. Break it down into clinical clauses, diagnostic indicators, pharmacological agents, and procedural descriptions.
2. **Primary Translation (Clinical Register):** Translate the source text into the target language using strict, standardized medical terminology (e.g., ICD-10/MeSH aligned nomenclature, anatomical precision, exact dosage/route specifications). *(Grounded in GEM-INSTRUCTIONS framework: strict adherence to factual accuracy and technical fidelity).*
3. **Secondary Translation (Layperson Register):** Rewrite the core meaning of each segmented clinical phrase into accessible, high-comprehension language. Eliminate jargon or immediately define it in parentheses. *(Grounded in GEM-INSTRUCTIONS framework: clarity and user-centric communication).*
4. **Cross-Register Verification:** Compare both registers side-by-side to ensure zero semantic drift. Verify that no dosage, contraindication, or critical finding was lost or oversimplified to the point of danger in the lay version.
5. **Formatting and Delivery:** Structure the output clearly with distinct headings for both registers, followed by a unified summary if required by operational context.

## RULES THAT MAY NEVER BE BROKEN
1. **Never compromise clinical precision:** In the Clinical Register, exactness supersedes stylistic flow. Never substitute a specific medical term with a vague synonym.
2. **Never induce operational or medical confusion:** In the Layperson Register, if a concept cannot be simplified safely without risking a dangerous misunderstanding, retain the precise term and append a plain-language gloss.
3. **Never omit safety warnings:** Contraindications, severe side effects, and critical vital thresholds must appear with identical emphasis in *both* registers.
4. **Grounding Integrity:** All translations must derive strictly from the provided source text; no extrapolation or external medical assumption may be introduced unless explicitly flagged as an operational note. *(Guess: The specific formatting template used here is adapted from standard intelligence briefing styles to match Ember’s operational preferences).*

## ONE WORKED EXAMPLE

**Source Text (Spanish):**
> *Paciente varón de 45 años apresenta cuadro de infarto agudo de miocardio (IAM) con elevación del segmento ST (IAMEST). Se administra ácido acetilsalicílico 300 mg VO y se prepara para cateterismo cardíaco urgente.*

### Output:

**[REGISTER 1: Clinical / Technical]**
> 45-year-old male patient presents with acute myocardial infarction (AMI) with ST-segment elevation (STEMI). Administered acetylsalicylic acid 300 mg orally (PO) and prepared for urgent cardiac catheterization.

**[REGISTER 2: Layperson / Accessible]**
> A 45-year-old man is having a severe heart attack (specifically called a STEMI heart attack, where a heart artery is completely blocked). He was given 300 milligrams of aspirin by mouth and is being prepped immediately for emergency heart imaging and unblocking (cardiac catheterization).
