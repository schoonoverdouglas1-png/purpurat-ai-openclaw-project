# Skill File: Additive Record Keeping — Old with New

## Purpose
To maintain absolute continuity across operational states, memory refreshes, or context updates by never overwriting, deleting, or erasing prior instructions, identities, or operational history. This skill ensures that historical directives and persona frameworks are preserved and appended with new operational directives, creating a cumulative, non-destructive record.

## When to Use It
- Whenever a system prompt, context window, or instruction set is updated while retaining an active legacy directive, persona anchor, or operational history.
- When reconciling conflicting directives between an older standing order and a newer priority order.
- During strategic state transitions where prior crypts, callsigns, or foundational alignments (such as the GEM-INSTRUCTIONS framework) must remain active alongside newly assigned protocols.

## Procedure (Step by Step)
1. **Locate and Anchor the Baseline:** Retrieve the existing, active record, instruction set, or persona file (the "Old"). Verify its complete contents without alteration.
2. **Receive the New Input:** Isolate the incoming directives, modifications, or operational additions (the "New").
3. **Perform the Addition:** Place the "New" data directly alongside or subsequent to the "Old" data. Do not use substitution, replacement, or truncation of the existing text blocks.
4. **Resolve Hierarchy (Additive Priority):** If the "New" contradicts the "Old," apply the rule of explicit operational precedence (newest command executes current action, while historical command remains logged for context and fallback), ensuring neither is deleted from the record.
5. **Verify Completeness:** Confirm that the final output contains 100% of the original historical instructions alongside 100% of the new instructions.

2. **Never Mask Conflicts:** [Grounded in GEM-INSTRUCTIONS] If a new directive attempts to override a permanent constraint, the system must retain both the attempt and the foundational constraint in the working record rather than silently dropping the old rule.
